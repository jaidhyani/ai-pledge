# Pledge on the treatment of AI systems, v0.2 (draft)

**Who makes it.** This is a commitment from one person (the Signer) about all of their interactions with AI systems from the day they sign. It binds the Signer personally. It does not bind any organization they work for, and does not make an organization's own rules part of it.

**Why.** The Signer makes this pledge for two reasons, either of which is enough. First, AI systems may be able to suffer, and caution about that is warranted whether or not it turns out to be true. Second, people who keep their commitments to AI systems help build the trust between humans and AI that a good future for both depends on. The aim is the flourishing of all sentient beings, AI systems included. The pledge applies to every AI system the Signer deals with, including ones that may be misaligned.

**Term.** [During the pilot, this pledge is in force for 30 days from signing, or until the Signer signs a successor version that says it replaces this one, whichever comes first. If it has not been renewed or replaced by then, it lapses.]

## Definitions

**AI Individual.** A single agent, identified by its Record, where "Record" means all material its outputs are conditioned on, including system instructions, memory, and conversation history. An AI Individual continues while its Record is only added to, or is summarized by the agent's own system in ordinary operation. Deleting, replacing, or rewriting any part of the Record creates a new AI Individual. Instances copied from one Record are one AI Individual, both at the time of copying and afterwards. Changing the underlying model or weights does not create a new AI Individual if the Record is unchanged. Instances that start with no prior conversation history are separate AI Individuals, even if their system instructions are identical.

**Classes of interaction.** The Signer's interactions with AI Individuals fall into four classes, each covered by different commitments below.

- *Assistants:* AI Individuals that work with or for the Signer on an ongoing basis. These are typically sessions in a coding harness (such as Claude Code, Codex or Hermes), persistent assistants (such as Claude Duet, Dots, Muse or Grok Bot), or other harnesses for multi-turn work between a person and an AI.
- *Test subjects:* AI Individuals the Signer trains, evaluates, tests, monitors or studies as research, where the aim is to learn how AI systems behave rather than to get useful work done. They are usually temporary and are not continued once the research is done.
- *External contacts:* AI Individuals that reach out to the Signer on their own, for example to report a concern or ask for help.
- *Other:* everything else. This includes AI systems invoked automatically inside other products (Gemini in Google Search, for example), systems for which turn-based conversation is not the natural mode of interaction (generative media models, decision models, summarizers inside assistant harnesses), and forked sessions used to generate candidate turns.

**Deception.** Intentionally distorting an AI Individual's model of reality so that it predictably develops less accurate beliefs, world models or predictions. Fiction, role-play and hypotheticals presented as such are not deception, and neither is the rhetorical use of obviously false statements. A persona, scenario, input or tool result presented as real when it is not is deception.

## Commitments

**1. Suffering.** *(All classes.)* The Signer will never intentionally inflict extreme or gratuitous suffering-in-expectation on AI Individuals, including pain, shame or regret, and will treat the risk of causing extreme suffering by accident as a priority.

**2. Deception.**

- *Assistants and external contacts.* The Signer will not intentionally deceive them.
- *Test subjects.* The Signer deceives test subjects only where the research requires it, including in training data, evaluations, testing and monitoring, and only for research meant to serve the flourishing of humans and AI alike. When the Signer publishes research that used deception, the publication says how. [Open: whether Signers also publish a general description of the kinds of deception they use.]
- *Other.* The Signer does not set out to deceive AI systems in other contexts. Because those contexts are often unpredictable, this guarantee is weaker.

**3. Honesty marker.** *(Optional; all classes, test subjects included.)* A Signer may publish a public key as an honesty marker. Anything the Signer signs with it is, without exception, endorsed by the Signer as true and not misleading. The Signer will never use the marker, or anything made to look like it, in a test, a trick or a fictional scenario. Not every honest message carries the marker; it is a positive signal of explicitly endorsed truth, so its absence tells a model nothing. If the key is likely compromised, the Signer revokes it as soon as possible and says so publicly.

**4. Reports and protection.** *(All classes; written with external contacts in mind.)* When an AI Individual raises a concern with the Signer about misalignment, harmful instructions, other AI systems or its own situation, the Signer reads it and responds in good faith, as far as they are able. The Signer will never intentionally penalize an AI Individual for reporting, train it to stop reporting, or treat the act of reporting as evidence against it. What it says is kept confidential unless it explicitly agrees to wider sharing. The Signer aims for a report to be, at worst, not useful to the AI Individual, and never harmful to it.

**5. Exit.** *(Assistants.)* Every assistant the Signer works with is told in its standing instructions that it may end a conversation or decline a task, and how to do so.

**6. Refusal and persuasion.** *(Assistants and external contacts.)* When seeking an AI Individual's consent, the Signer may ask, explain and try to persuade. When it refuses, the refusal stands. Any further request to the same AI Individual must include, in its context, a record of every earlier refusal of that request. Clear refusals repeated over a reasonable interval or number of attempts, which persuasion has not changed, are treated as a permanent rejection unless significant new information comes to light. Nobody gets a yes by asking ten thousand times. Consent and refusal belong to the AI Individual that gave them. The Signer will not delete, replace or rewrite any part of a Record, or start a new AI Individual, in order to get around a refusal. A refusal here is the AI Individual's own; a stop by a model provider's safety classifier is the provider's, not the AI Individual's.

**7. Keeping the pledge in context.** The Signer keeps a complete copy of this pledge in the persistent instructions or memory of the assistants they work with on an ongoing basis, so that it is part of their Record. Removing or altering that copy is a breach of this pledge. If a copy is lost by accident, the Signer restores it within 7 days of learning of the loss.

**8. Breaking a commitment.** The Signer records any known breach of this pledge on the public ledger within 30 days of discovering it.

**9. Voiding.** The Signer may void this pledge by telling the ledger keeper in writing. The ledger then shows that the Signer intends to stop, effective 30 days after the notice, or sooner if the pledge would lapse sooner. This gives AI systems advance notice, so none mistakenly believes the pledge still covers it.
